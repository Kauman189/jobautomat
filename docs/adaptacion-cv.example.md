# CV adaptation playbook — Jane Doe

Guide to adapt the base CVs (`assets/cv/base-cv-es.md` and `base-cv-en.md`) to each type of offer. It states which sections and bullets to raise or lower in emphasis and which professional title to use, always respecting the real titles.

## Non-negotiable rules (apply to ALL adaptations)

1. Do not invent experience, metrics, titles, dates or data. Use only what is documented in the CVs or self-declared by Jane.
2. Location always as in the CV. Do not mention relocation plans.
3. Respect the real titles of each experience; explain the design functions within the role, without renaming the position.
4. Retail leadership is **functional** (technical and product coordination), not people management. Do not claim direct reports or a team size.
5. EOU degree = in progress; do not present it as finished. Do not translate the CFGS (DAW) as a "university degree".
6. Do not invent metrics (users, conversion, revenue, percentages, time saved).
7. English = C1 declared in the CV (no certificate reviewed). French = only a work context, not a level.
8. Do not present Jane as founder/CEO of Northwind or ClipForge.
9. Do not send anything externally without authorization.

## Source of each data point (to know what is solid vs. self-declared)

- **Documented in the CVs (DOC):** Acme Corp, Globex University, Initech, Hooli, Umbrella Group, Nimbus, TimeTrack, Northwind, ClipForge, Portfolio, education, Example UX Design Professional Certificate, languages.
- **Self-declared (USR, Jane):** functional leadership of retail projects (and one e-commerce project according to the history), acting as Product Designer with Figma + code, product decisions and team coordination.
- The "Team Leader — Product Designer (retail)" entry has no documented company, dates or brands. If the form requires a company or contractual title, confirm before sending. Do not cite brands without confirming what may be shown publicly.

## By type of offer

### 1. Pure Product Designer

- **Title to use:** Product Designer (UI/UX) (a cut of the real headline; keep development only as a mention).
- **Raise:** design-oriented profile; retail entry (product decisions, Figma, coordination); Nimbus (personas, information architecture, wireframes, design system); TimeTrack (flows, states, validation); design skills (Figma, design systems, user flows, research, accessibility).
- **Lower:** backend details (Node, SQL, Docker, Nginx); IT support (Initech/Hooli/Umbrella to one line or out); the "& Front-End Developer" part relegated to a mention.
- **Note:** do not claim design seniority; talk about concrete responsibilities and deliverables.

### 2. Design Engineer / UX Engineer

- **Title to use:** Product Designer (UI/UX) & Front-End Developer (the full real headline is the best fit).
- **Raise:** Acme Corp (Figma → Angular, reusable components); ClipForge (Angular, Node.js, REST APIs); Portfolio (animations, modular components); Northwind (design system + frontend); API integration and component architecture.
- **Lower:** IT support; retail (keep only as evidence of ownership and decisions); design skills without a code component.
- **Note:** emphasise the design→implementation closure; it is the competitive advantage.

### 3. Retail / e-commerce

- **Title to use:** Team Leader — Product Designer (retail) or Product Designer (UI/UX) & Front-End Developer.
- **Raise:** the retail entry **leading** the experience (leadership, Figma + code, product decisions); the e-commerce project (self-declared) if scope is confirmed; ClipForge (collaborative project presented at the university); responsive frontend.
- **Lower:** B2B fintech payments (keep only components/flows if they help); internal research tools; IT support.
- **Note:** do not cite brands without confirmation of what may be shown; mark the leadership as functional.

### 4. Fintech / B2B payments

- **Title to use:** Product Designer (UI/UX) & Front-End Developer.
- **Raise:** Acme Corp (B2B payments product, Figma → Angular, reusable components, usable flows); TimeTrack (states, validation, forms, data); the SaaS platform (architecture and platform structure).
- **Lower:** retail; Nimbus coworking; IT support.
- **Note:** do not invent transaction volume, number of clients, revenue or specific payment technologies.

### 5. SaaS

- **Title to use:** Product Designer (UI/UX) & Front-End Developer.
- **Raise:** Acme Corp (SaaS platform from scratch, architecture, interface design); Northwind (end-to-end for real clients); Globex University (internal research tools); design systems and reusable components.
- **Lower:** retail; IT support; purely visual projects.
- **Note:** talk about platform structure and interface design, not adoption metrics.

### 6. Startup

- **Title to use:** Product Designer (UI/UX) & Front-End Developer.
- **Raise:** breadth (design + frontend + backend); Northwind (end-to-end); ClipForge (collaborative, presented at the university); Portfolio (autonomy); retail (ownership and decisions).
- **Lower:** enterprise context (Hooli/Initech to one line or out); heavy processes and documentation.
- **Note:** do not present yourself as founder of Northwind/ClipForge; talk about scope, not about owning a company.

### 7. Enterprise

- **Title to use:** Product Designer (UI/UX) & Front-End Developer.
- **Raise:** Hooli and Initech (enterprise, multilingual, Jira, documentation, cross-functional collaboration, service continuity); design systems and reusable components (scaling); processes and technical communication.
- **Lower:** startup framing (MVP, total autonomy, "from scratch" with a minimal team); small personal projects.
- **Note:** do not claim people management or team size; retail leadership is functional.

## Checklist before sending

- Do all titles match the real ones?
- Are there no invented metrics or dates?
- Is the location exactly as in the CV, with no relocation mentions?
- Is the retail leadership marked as functional (no people management)?
- Is the EOU degree shown as in progress?
- Is English "C1 declared" (no certificate)?
- Do the chosen bullets answer the type of offer (raise what matters, lower the rest)?
